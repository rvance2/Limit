# Sources

Graded by how much weight I give them. Tier 1: peer-reviewed, sample worth caring about. Tier 2: large-N practitioner data, or peer-reviewed with a small sample. Tier 3: expert opinion and mechanism, no direct climbing data.

## Verification notes

I checked primaries where I could reach them. Two things to remember.

Some numbers in this literature live only inside figure images, so a fetch can't read them. Where I've relied on someone else's transcription of a figure, the note says so — [[Rate of Force Development]], [[Finger Strength]], [[Benchmarks]].

CAMP4's research summaries are a good index and a bad quote source. Several contain unit-conversion errors, and the Stien 2021 write-up contradicts itself on time-to-peak-force. Use them to find papers.

## Tier 1 — peer-reviewed

- Stien N, Vereide VA, Saeterbakken AH, Hermans E, Shaw MP, Andersen V (2021). Upper body rate of force development and maximal strength discriminates performance levels in sport climbing. *PLOS ONE* 16(3): e0249353. n=57. https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0249353
- Stien N et al. (2022). Differences in Upper-Body Peak Force and Rate of Force Development in Male Intermediate, Advanced, and Elite Sport Climbers. *Frontiers in Sports and Active Living*. n=78 across three IRCRA bands. **The paper that reweighted this plan** — RFD +74.9% advanced→elite against +45.5% for the step before, while peak force climbs a flat ~40% per band. Sport climbing, so bouldering transfer is assumed. Reports the intermediate→advanced RFD p-value as 0.046 in the abstract and 0.057 in the discussion. https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2022.888061/full

Dense with mechanistic technique detail the literature doesn't contain. Extracted in [[Burden of Dreams — Wheeler and Shriber]]; sourced ideas are flagged as Tier 3 wherever they appear.
- "Hoseok Lee style" pull training. No published protocol exists. What circulates is reconstructed from his public posts — heavy low-rep weighted pull-ups, high-frequency one-arm lock-off holds at multiple joint angles, one-arm negatives. He is among the strongest pullers in climbing and it plainly works for him, which is not the same as a programme with a sample size. Basis for [[One Arm Progression]], flagged as inference in that note.
- One-arm pull-up prerequisite heuristic (weighted pull-up 1RM ≈ bodyweight + 50-65%). Calisthenics community lore, widely repeated, no research I could find. See [[Benchmarks]].
