# Benchmarks

What the numbers mean, and which of these are real.

## Finger strength — two-arm 7 s hang on 20 mm, % bodyweight including bodyweight

Lattice's published points, male climbers:

| Grade | % bodyweight |
| --- | --- |
| V4 | 128% |
| V7 | 146% |
| V11 | 170% |

Those three are Lattice's. The gaps interpolate at ~6% per grade, and Tom Randall has confirmed the general linearity in interview. So V8 ≈ 152%, V9 ≈ 158%, V10 ≈ 164%.

**Interpolated numbers are inference, not measurement.** Probably close. Not data.

Also: averages, describing the *maximum* grade of a climber with that strength, not the grade they climb consistently. "V9 finger strength" may mean exactly one V9.

## My band

V8-V10 on the two-arm table is 152% → 164% of bodyweight. About 12% of bodyweight across the band, or roughly 8-9 kg at 70 kg. That's the size of the gap in the measure that best predicts grade.

Pair that with finger strength explaining ~17% of variance at Advanced and ~10% at Elite. Closing 12% of bodyweight is necessary and nowhere near sufficient. [[Factors MOC]].

## Finger strength — one-arm pull on 20 mm, % bodyweight

Lattice's published points:

| Grade | % bodyweight |
| --- | --- |
| V14 | 106% |
| V15 | 110% |
| V16 | 114% |
| V17 | 118% |

Linear 4% per grade at the top.

The circulated table running V4 at 49% to V17 at 118% is a blogger's extrapolation. It takes the 6% per-grade increases from the two-arm data and applies them to one-arm pulls, assuming 5% per grade between V11 and V14 because that falls between the observed 6% and 4%. Possibly roughly right. V8 = 73% and V9 = 79% are plausible targets, not facts, and the author flagged the biggest errors as being at the ends of the range.

## Pulling strength

Lattice report 2RM as % bodyweight but I didn't find their grade-by-grade table published. What I know is the predictive weight: R = 0.582 combined, adj R² 0.117 Advanced, 0.046 Elite, not significant above V13.

Practical read: a 2RM pull-up with roughly 30-40% bodyweight added puts me in the range where pulling strength isn't my limiter. **Below that, pulling plausibly is one of my limiters and the 12% bites hard — that's the case for the pull work in [[S6 Skill and Pull]].** Above it, further pulling buys very little grade and becomes a goal pursued for its own sake. Test it in week 0 before deciding how many hours it deserves.

## One-arm pull-up prerequisite

Community heuristic: a strict one-arm pull-up needs a weighted pull-up 1RM around bodyweight + 50-65%.

No research behind this. Calisthenics lore, widely repeated, plausible on leverage grounds, never tested that I can find. Tier 3.

Useful as a direction rather than a threshold. If my 1RM is well under +50% BW, the honest next move is more [[Weighted Pulls]] and less rushing [[One Arm Progression]] stages 4-6.

## One-arm lock-off

No published norms exist. Targets from [[One Arm Progression]], which are programme targets and not population data:

| Angle | Target hold, per side |
| --- | --- |
| Full lock, assisted | 8 s |
| Full lock, unassisted | 10 s |
| 90° elbow | 8 s |
| 120° elbow | 5 s |

Compare against my own previous number, per side, and watch the asymmetry.

## Power endurance

Don't benchmark it. Lattice's 60%-of-max repeaters-to-exhaustion test correlated with best boulder worked at R = 0.007 across 901 climbers, p = 0.830. Predicted nothing at any ability level. [[Critical Force]].

## Flexibility

Box split explains ~2% of grade variance combined and nothing within ability groups. No meaningful benchmark. Use my week-0 number and aim not to lose range.

## Elite reference points, for scale

Stien et al. (2021), n=57 (17 intermediate, 25 advanced, 15 elite), maximal isometric pull-up force on 23 mm at 90° elbow:

- Elite above advanced, ES = 1.78, p < 0.001.
- Elite above intermediate, ES = 1.77, p < 0.001.
- Intermediate vs advanced: no difference, p = 0.898.

Force isn't a smooth ramp across ability. Flat, then it jumps. The gap I'm trying to close isn't linear, which is one reason grade progression stalls and then moves.

Same pattern for RFD, sharper: elite exceeded both groups at all relative time windows, and on absolute windows only at 50, 100 and 150 ms. Past 150 ms the differences wash out.

A secondary summary gives group means of roughly 2519 / 1272 / 1166 N and 1457 / 799 / 801 N/s at 50 ms. Those live in figure images rather than the text — indicative magnitudes, not quotable numbers. [[Sources]].

## How to use all of this

Compare against my week-0 self, not the tables. The tables tell me roughly where the population sits and which of my numbers is furthest behind the others. That second thing is the useful part: if my pull-up percentile sits well above my finger-strength percentile, I know where the next 22 weeks go.
