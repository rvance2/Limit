# Block 4 — Peak and Send

Three weeks. Volume down hard, intensity held, everything pointed at the boulder. This block doesn't build anything; it lets what I built show up.

Three rather than four, because Block 3 grew. The send window is effectively ten weeks anyway — outdoor projecting starts at week 13.

## The taper, and the mistake I'd make

- Cut volume 40-60%. Some reviews put the useful range at 41-70%.
- **Maintain or slightly increase intensity.** Reductions of 30-60% decreased performance by 20-30% in the reviewed studies. In power athletes, maintaining intensity up to the performance date preserved and improved 1RM and jump performance.

So: keep doing near-maximal hangs, do far fewer of them. The instinct to rest properly by doing nothing hard is precisely backwards — I'd arrive detrained, feeling fresh, and weaker. If I feel flat in this block, I cut intensity instead of volume. [[Checkpoints]].

## Conditions become the schedule

[[Conditions Forecasting]] drives when I go, not the calendar. Dew point, rock temperature and aspect, wind. Three of five go/no-go inputs bad → train instead, go tomorrow.

Fresh shoes broken in during week 20 and saved. Fan packed. Frozen bottle if hot. Brush every three or four attempts.

## Skin is the binding constraint

On sharp rock, attempt count is skin-limited before it's strength-limited. Follow the [[Skin Programme]] Antihydral schedule with a Friday target. The attempt budget in [[Attempt Discipline]] is partly a skin budget.

Skin at 2/5 on a project day: go anyway and take four attempts rather than ten. Four good attempts on good skin beats ten degrading ones.

## After

Don't roll straight into another 22 weeks. Two weeks unstructured, then review the log, then choose the next limiter on evidence rather than on whichever weakness feels most annoying.
