# Block 3 — Power and RFD

Six weeks. The longest block in the plan and the one the reweighting was for.

## Why this is now the biggest block

Stien et al. 2022, n=78, three IRCRA bands on an isometric pull-up:

| Measure | Intermediate → Advanced | Advanced → Elite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Peak force | +40.1% | +39.7% |
| **RFD** | +45.5% | **+74.9%** |

Peak force climbs at a flat ~40% per band. RFD nearly doubles into elite. The authors read it as RFD becoming an increasingly important limiting factor at elite grades, because those grades feature steeper terrain and longer moves.

At V8-V10 I'm crossing exactly that boundary, and RFD is the thing I have the least equipment for. Four weeks was not enough. [[Factors MOC]].

Caveat: that's sport climbing, IRCRA 24-27 ≈ French 8a-8b+. Transfer to bouldering is assumed, not shown.

## Priorities in order

1. [[Deadpoint Power]] — twice weekly. Full ladder including foot-cuts and dynos.
2. [[Recruitment Pulls]] — twice weekly, maximum intent, every rep measured.
3. [[Limit Bouldering]] — twice weekly, now often outdoors.
4. [[Contact Strength Ladder]] — stages 5-7.
5. [[Max Hangs]] — maintenance, 3 sets, twice weekly.
6. [[One Arm Progression]] — stages 6-7. Negatives, then assisted one-arm pull-ups.
7. [[Weighted Pulls]] — maintenance, 4 × 4 @ 82-85%.

## Why finger volume drops

The target changed. Rate work and peak-force work compete for the same nervous system and the same recovery. [[Max Hangs]] at three sets maintains what Block 2 built while freeing capacity for the rate work.

## Frequency

Two dedicated power sessions a week, not four. The campus board trial: four weekly sessions improved RFD and maximal force, two improved bouldering performance and moves-to-failure, at matched volume. Four sessions of explosive work leaves me unable to climb well, and climbing well is what makes me better at climbing.

## Fatigue management — this block needs it

Six weeks is the longest stretch between reduced weeks in the plan, and it's the explosive block. Neural fatigue shows up as reduced RFD *before* reduced peak force, so it's nearly invisible.

**Regulation rule:** if [[Readiness Monitoring]] hits 2+ flags for three consecutive sessions, insert a light week at week 16 and shorten the block to five. Losing a week beats losing the block.

## Outdoor transition

[[S5 Outdoor Project Day]] replaces [[S2 Limit Boulder]] whenever conditions allow. [[Conditions Forecasting]] becomes a scheduling input.

Two things to start: [[Beta Lab]] on the project, session one beta-only; and [[Lower Limb Resilience]] approach loading two weeks before any trip with real approaches.

## Checkpoint

The hardest block to feel. RFD improvements don't announce themselves — there's no sensation attached to producing force 40 ms sooner. The only signal is [[Recruitment Pulls]] session-best peak trending up. Noisy line, upward trend. [[Checkpoints]].

## Week 19 is reduced

60% volume, no power work, outdoor easy mileage. Skin recovers here, which matters because Block 4 is skin-expensive.

## Exit criteria

- [[Recruitment Pulls]] peak above the week-7 figure.
- [[One Arm Progression]] at stage 7, assisted one-arm pull-up, both sides.
- A project selected with a written beta from [[Beta Lab]].
