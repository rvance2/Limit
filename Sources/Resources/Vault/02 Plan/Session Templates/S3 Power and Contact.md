# S3 Power and Contact

Rate work. Dies fastest under fatigue, and most often wasted by doing it at 90% intent. Also the week's second [[Max Hangs]] / [[MED Hangs]] session — 48 h after Monday's, which is the spacing that actually matters for finger recovery.

Primary finger method is the same block-conditional resolution as [[S1 Finger Priority]] — B1/B3/B4: [[Max Hangs]], B2: [[MED Hangs]]. Week 22 tapers to once weekly, Monday only; skip it here that week.

In Block 1 this becomes a second [[S4 Volume and Skill]] with [[Contact Strength Ladder]] stage 2 added — finger method stays, since Block 1's whole point is the finger base.

No dedicated [[Shoulder Protocol]] here — the comprehensive session is Saturday, light activation is Thursday and Sunday. Loading the cuff hard the day before or after this session's catch work would be working the same tissue twice in close succession for no real gain.

## Intent requirement

RFD responds to maximal intent, not load. A rep at 90% trains something other than rate. The campus board researchers used a live force readout specifically because they didn't trust trained athletes to produce maximal force on verbal instruction. Neither should I.

Two maximal efforts beat six nearly maximal ones. If I can't tell the difference, that's the argument for a load cell.

## Frequency logic

Two dedicated power sessions a week, not four. The campus board trial found four weekly sessions built RFD and maximal force while two built bouldering performance and moves-to-failure, at matched volume. The frequency that built the quality wasn't the frequency that built the performance. Four sessions of explosive work leaves me unable to climb well, and climbing well is what makes me better at climbing.

## Progression logic

No shoulder-based restrictions on dynos or one-arm catches — bars and edges have never been a problem. [[Shoulder Capacity]]. Progression through [[Deadpoint Power]] stages is general load management: advance on clean landings, not on a calendar.

## Stop rules

- Grip position changing mid-hang: that set doesn't count. Two in a row ends the exercise.
- Palpation tenderness over a pulley found during warm-up: 25-30 mm and drop load 30% for the day, then read [[Tendon Health]].
- Catch quality degrading — sloppy landings, uncontrolled swing — means the nervous system is done. Over regardless of reps remaining.
- [[Recruitment Pulls]]: peak 5% below session best.
- Shoulder pain: exercise over for the day. The painless positional click doesn't appear in catch work.
- Any finger pop, click, or sting: session over.
