## Setting limit boulders in a commercial gym

Build them from what exists. One variable at a time:

- Boulder two grades above my limit, topout deleted. Work moves 3-5 only.
- Boulder at my limit, one foothold removed, or one intermediate.
- Link the crux of one boulder into the crux of an adjacent one.
- Steepest wall available. Steep makes moves harder through force and tension rather than fingertip precision on tiny gym crimps — the kind of hard I want, and it spares [[Skin]].
- Sit starts. Cheapest difficulty multiplier in any gym.

## The mindset problem

If I send it, it wasn't a limit boulder. Hampton is direct: the mindset that requires topping out every try is what stops people doing this exercise correctly, and some of his most productive training seasons involved not sending a single hard boulder in the gym.

Expect to look bad. Bring [[Attempt Discipline]] and follow it.

## Stop rules

- Three consecutive attempts worse than the session best. Over, whatever the clock says.
- Any fingertip hot spot. Over. Skin costs a week and the remaining attempts are low quality.
- Any move that makes me think about my shoulder gets abandoned, not repeated.
- Never on the same day as [[Recruitment Pulls]] at full intensity.
