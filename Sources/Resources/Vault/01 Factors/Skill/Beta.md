# Beta

The sequence. On a limit boulder better beta is usually cheaper than more strength, and always faster to acquire.

## The core claim

I converge on a sequence in the first two or three attempts, then spend fifteen sessions trying to get strong enough for it. That sequence got chosen when I knew least about the boulder.

Coaching observation, not a research finding, and the pattern I'd bet on in any stalled V8. The counter is procedural: force alternatives before committing.

## What selection depends on

- [[Morphology]] and [[Height and Span]]. Different bodies need different sequences. Tall-person beta isn't *the* beta.
- [[Flexibility and Mobility]]. A sequence needing a high step I can't make isn't available.
- [[Leverage]]. Most beta improvements are vector improvements — same holds, different orientation.
- [[Friction]] and conditions. Some beta only works when friction is good. Knowing which of my options is the low-friction option matters on the day.
- My strengths. Crimp-strong and sloper-strong climbers should pick different intermediates.

## Protocol

[[Beta Lab]] formalises it: three sequences minimum per crux before committing, each written down, each tried twice, one assuming a different body. Then pick, and give it a fixed session budget before re-opening the question.

The written-down part matters. Beta held in my head degrades between sessions and I re-derive the sequence I already rejected.

## Replicas and boards

Worth building the crux at home? For a specific project with a specific crux, yes, and the top end does it. No systems board here, which limits reproducing a crux, but I can usually approximate the *movement pattern* on gym holds. Enough for the coordination, not enough for the specific finger position.
