# Footwork

If my feet are unreliable, every hand move becomes a strength move. That's the whole argument.

No controlled trial on climbing footwork drills, so this is mechanism plus coaching consensus. The mechanism isn't controversial — a foot that holds turns my legs into force producers, and legs are stronger than fingers.

## Four failures

1. **Placement inaccuracy.** Foot lands off the best part and needs adjusting. Every adjustment costs hand time and friction.
2. **Insufficient load.** Foot is on the hold but I'm not pushing through it, so it's decoration. Very common on steep ground.
3. **Lost vector.** Foot loaded but pushing the wrong direction, so it peels. [[Leverage]].
4. **Unintentional cut loose.** Usually a [[Tension]] failure, sometimes foothold selection.

## The foot is a force producer, not a placeholder

Failure 2 above has a mechanism worth spelling out, from [[Burden of Dreams — Wheeler and Shriber]].

On a sloped foothold with the heel down, most climbers leave the foot **passive** during the move. Actively plantarflexing — driving the toe down, engaging the calf — does two things:

1. Exerts downward force, which **offloads the hands before the move starts**. Fingers supporting less weight can move faster.
2. Locks a weight-bearing joint, which adds stability.

The second half is more counterintuitive: **drop the body as I reach.** Pushing down naturally sends the body up, so instead of going up toward the hold, actively move bodyweight *down into the foot*. That forces more weight into the foothold at the exact moment it wants to slip.

The reframe: change the objective from *get the hand up* to *get the hand while driving weight into the foot*.

Related detail from the same session — on a move where the free leg swings out and peels me off, bring the flying leg close to the other leg rather than letting it flag wide. It reverses the direction it pulls at the end of the move.

Rehearse this slowly in [[Focal Point Warm-Up]] drill 4 before trying to apply it on a limit move.

## Drills per failure

- Silent feet, one or two grades below limit. Audible contact = failed rep. Fixes 1.
- Target practice: pick the exact square centimetre before moving, eyes on it until the shoe lands, don't look away early. Hörst's version is the cleanest description. Fixes 1.
- No-adjust: once it lands it stays. Two to three grades below limit. Fixes 1 aggressively.
- Toe tap: on an overhang, repeatedly tap a small hold before committing. Accuracy and toe strength together. Fixes 1 and 2.
- Downclimb everything I top out. Forces footholds I didn't choose, blind. Fixes 1 and 3.
- Foot-on traverses with deliberate over-loading of each foot before the hand moves. Fixes 2.

## The one that matters most

Downclimbing, because of [[Motor Learning]]. High contextual interference — different task every time, novel positions forced, no pre-planning. Random practice degrades in-session performance and improves retention and transfer, which is the trade I want for a skill I need in nine weeks rather than tonight.

## Programming

Twelve minutes at the start of every [[S4 Volume and Skill]] session, easy terrain, before fatigue. Footwork drilled tired trains sloppy footwork.
