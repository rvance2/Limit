# Commitment

Executing a move where hesitation guarantees failure. Distinct from [[Intent]] — intent is how hard I pull, commitment is whether I go at all.

## Also a physical problem

On a bad crimp or a poor foot, holding the position costs force. Deliberating there spends the force the move needed. So committing early often succeeds on strength the hesitant version also had and spent on thinking.

That reframes commitment from a courage question to a resource-allocation question. More useful, less moralising.

## The falling piece

MacLeod's argument: fear of falling is the largest single impediment to climbing performance, and physical training doesn't touch it. On boulders the fear is smaller than on lead but not zero — the fall from a crux with a bad landing, the fall where a foot might catch, the heel hook I won't weight because of how I'd come off.

His approach, worth adopting: rather than fighting fear, address the specific impulses creating it while staying oriented to the satisfaction of doing the move. Systematic, not heroic.

## Training it

[[Commitment Reps]]. Graded exposure with the outcome removed:

1. Identify the specific move I won't commit to.
2. Make the consequence trivially safe. More pads, a spotter, lower the move, closer to the ground.
3. Execute at full commitment ten times in the safe version.
4. Restore one element of consequence. Repeat.
5. Restore the real version.

Step 3 is repetition of the *decision*, not the movement. Training the go, not the go's mechanics.

## What doesn't work

Talking myself into it in the moment. By the time I'm in the position the decision needs to already be made. Deciding on the wall is what hesitation is.
