# Visualization

The one mental skill with a framework, a protocol, and intervention studies. If I train one thing in [[Mind MOC]], this and [[Patience]]. Module: [[Visualization Protocol]].

## PETTLEP

Holmes and Collins (2001), built on functional equivalence — imagery should recruit as much of the same neural substrate as execution as possible. Seven elements:

- **Physical** — adopt the actual posture. Shoes on. Hands chalked. Hold the position if I can.
- **Environment** — at the real place, or looking at a photo. Not on the sofa.
- **Task** — the specific task at my level, not an idealised version.
- **Timing** — real time. A four-second move takes four seconds.
- **Learning** — update as the skill changes. Yesterday's image of the crux is wrong today.
- **Emotion** — include the arousal and the nerves. Sanitised imagery doesn't transfer.
- **Perspective** — internal generally beats external, though external is useful for checking body position.

A systematic review of PETTLEP-based motor imagery found positive performance effects, larger at higher imagery frequency. Evidence also exists for strength gains and pain management from imagery built on the model. The 2022 twenty-year update extends it toward simulation-based training.

## Protocol

Two minutes twice a day, plus once between attempts.

Standing, shoes on if convenient. Eyes closed. Real time. Internal. Feel the crimp's edge on my finger pads, the specific angle of the foot, the moment of commitment. Include the sensation of trying hard and the nerves before pulling on.

Between attempts at the crag: same, in position under the boulder, hands chalked, reaching for the start holds without weighting them.

## Where it earns its keep

**Skin destroyed.** Practice with zero skin cost. On day three of a four-day trip that's worth more than another attempt.

**Can't get to the boulder.** Video plus imagery keeps the motor plan alive between trips.

**Immediately before the good attempt.** One rehearsal in real time, then go. Not five — five becomes rumination.

## What it won't do

Build force. It won't make a crimp I can't hold holdable. It sharpens execution of movements I can nearly do, which is exactly the limit-boulder situation.
