# Patience

Project-session tactics: how many attempts, how long between, when to stop, how many sessions before I change plan. My biggest untrained factor. Unglamorous, which is why it stayed untrained.

## Physiology sets the floor

PCr drives maximal efforts. Resynthesis largely complete 1-2 min, functionally complete 3-5. At 60 s I'm at ~85%.

So: 3-5 min between limit attempts. Not "when I feel ready" — I feel ready at 90 s and I'm not. Timer.

Knock-on: a real limit session has few attempts in it. Six to ten quality attempts across two or three problems at five-minute rests = 60-90 min. That's the session. Twenty-five attempts in the same window is twenty-five attempts at 70-85%, which trains something, not power and not the boulder.

## Skin sets the ceiling

A split takes a week. Muscles ready Wednesday, skin Thursday. On sharp crimps skin is the binding constraint and attempts are currency I'm spending. [[Skin]].

## Session-level discipline

Rules, not suggestions:

1. Timer between attempts. Three minutes minimum, five on the crux.
2. Fixed attempt budget, decided before the session, not during.
3. Stop after three consecutive attempts worse than my best of the day. Performance decline means the useful part is over regardless of remaining time.
4. Log everything. Attempt number, highest point, genuine or not, one line on why it failed.

## Block-level discipline

The other half is not changing plan too fast. Lattice's finger-training rule — one or two methods for six to twelve weeks before assessing. Same for beta: once chosen in [[Beta Lab]], give it a fixed number of sessions before re-opening. Three is usually right.

Method-hopping and beta-hopping feel like progress. They're the main way I'd train for years without changing.
