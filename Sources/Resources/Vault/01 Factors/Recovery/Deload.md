# Deload

I wanted no stops. This note is why the plan has reduced weeks anyway, and why they aren't stops.

## Mechanism

Strength adaptation isn't caused by training. It's caused by recovering from training. During a hard block I accumulate fatigue faster than I clear it, so measured performance goes *down* while adaptive potential goes up. A reduced week lets fatigue clear faster than adaptation decays, and the performance surfaces.

Tissue makes it non-optional. Muscle recovers in days, tendon and pulley in weeks. A block with no reduced weeks doesn't produce a stronger climber in week 12; it produces one whose fingers are two weeks behind his forearms, and that gap is where injuries live. [[Tendon Health]].

## Reduced, not off

Every session slot stays. What changes:

- Finger load 60% of the previous week's volume, intensity held at 85-90%.
- Limit bouldering replaced by boulders 2-3 grades below limit, focus on [[Movement Efficiency]].
- Power work removed for the week.
- [[Shoulder Protocol]], [[Hip and Ankle Mobility]], [[Footwork Precision]], [[Visualization Protocol]] unchanged. These are the weeks to make progress on them.
- Skin heals. [[Skin]].

Four to five days of climbing, less of the damaging part. That's the whole intervention.

## The taper, a bigger version of the same thing

Peak block uses taper principles from the strength and power literature. Consistent finding: cut volume, hold intensity.

- Volume reduction 40-60% is the common recommendation; some reviews put the range at 41-70%.
- Intensity must be maintained or slightly increased. Reductions of 30-60% decreased aerobic and anaerobic performance by 20-30% in the reviewed studies.
- In power athletes, maintaining intensity up to the performance date preserved and improved 1RM and jump performance.

Translated: in [[Block 4 — Peak and Send]] I keep doing near-maximal hangs and do far fewer of them. The instinct to "rest properly" before a trip by doing nothing hard is precisely wrong.

## Schedule

Reduced weeks 6, 12, 19. A 5:1, 5:1, then 6:1 pattern. Week 22 is the taper.

The 6:1 stretch is weeks 13-18, which is also the explosive block — the worst place to have the longest gap. Regulation rule: if [[Readiness Monitoring]] hits 2+ flags for three consecutive sessions, insert a light week at 16 and shorten [[Block 3 — Power and RFD]] to five. Losing a week beats losing the block.

If I arrive at a reduced week feeling great, take it anyway. Feeling great in week 6 of a hard block usually means week 6 was easier than intended.
