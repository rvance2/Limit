# Fatigue

Four kinds, four timescales, four responses. Treating them as one thing is why "I'm tired" is useless information.

## The four

**Phosphagen depletion.** Minutes. PCr resynthesis largely complete 1-2 min, functionally complete 3-5. At 60 s I'm at ~85%. Response: rest longer between attempts. [[Patience]].

**Peripheral forearm.** Tens of minutes to hours. Local metabolite accumulation, reduced force capacity in the flexors. Measurable via [[Critical Force]] with a load cell. Response: end the finger-intensive part; other work may still be productive.

**Neural.** Hours to days. Reduced maximal voluntary drive, and reduced [[Rate of Force Development]] *before* reduced peak force. Ruins power sessions and is nearly invisible — I feel fine and my first-100 ms force is down. Response: 48 h minimum between maximal power sessions, no RFD work after limit bouldering.

**Tissue.** Days to weeks. Tendon, pulley, capsule. Signals: morning stiffness, palpation tenderness, warm-up taking longer than last week. Response: reduce load 30% for a week. [[Tendon Health]].

## The one that ruins plans

Tissue, because its timescale is longer than my attention span. I feel good, train hard, and the tissue accumulates a debt that shows up in week 10 as a pulley strain. The reduced weeks exist mostly for this. [[Deload]].

## Monitoring load

Session RPE for internal load: rate 1-10, multiply by minutes. Sum for the week = acute. Four-week average = chronic. Acute-to-chronic ratio has a proposed safe zone of 0.8-1.3.

Honest about ACWR: criticised as methodologically flawed and misused, injury-prediction results mixed. I use it because the *direction* is sound — a week far heavier than recent average is a risk — not because 1.3 is a real threshold.

The monitoring literature prefers multi-indicator: HRV, session RPE, sleep, and skill-specific load together. That's [[Readiness Monitoring]].

## Rule of thumb

Two of these true on a training morning → downgrade: HRV more than about 10% below baseline, sleep under seven hours, resting HR up more than five beats, motivation flat, any finger stiff on waking. Three → skill and mobility. Four → rest.
