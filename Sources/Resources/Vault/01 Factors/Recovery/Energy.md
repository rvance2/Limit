# Energy

Fuelling. The part of the plan most likely to get quietly sabotaged by climbing culture's relationship with bodyweight.

## Energy systems for four moves

Almost entirely phosphagen. ATP-PCr produces ATP faster than anything else and dominates efforts up to ~10 s. Resynthesis largely complete 1-2 min, functionally complete 3-5; at 60 s rest I'm at ~85%.

Consequence: glycolytic and oxidative systems aren't what limit the attempt. They limit the *twentieth* attempt. Carbohydrate availability across a long project session matters for the same reason [[Movement Efficiency]] does — session durability, not single-attempt output.

## Carbohydrate

Don't train limit boulders low-carb. High-intensity work depends on carbohydrate, and impaired carbohydrate utilisation is a documented consequence of chronic under-fuelling.

1-1.5 g/kg in the two to three hours before a hard session, 30-60 g/h during long project sessions. Nothing exotic.

## Caffeine, and a result I don't like

A 2026 RCT gave 3 mg/kg to intermediate-advanced climbers and tested pull-up and grip performance. No statistically or practically meaningful ergogenic effect on any measured variable. Participants reported significantly greater subjective strength, energy and alertness.

Clean and deflating: caffeine at that dose made climbers feel stronger without making them stronger. The general resistance-training literature does support 3-6 mg/kg for strength and power, so this may be dose, specificity, or a small-sample null. But the only climbing-specific RCT says the effect is subjective.

How I'll use it: arousal and alertness on an early project day, because feeling capable has value on a limit boulder. No performance increment budgeted. And not before an afternoon session if it costs me the night — the sleep is worth more. [[Sleep]].

## RED-S

The reason to be careful with the weight conversation. Documented consequences: reduced bone mineral density and microstructure, impaired carbohydrate utilisation, endocrine disruption, and in male athletes failure to achieve expected bone mineral accrual. Performance declines.

Climbing selects for low bodyweight and rewards it short-term, which makes the sport structurally prone to this. If I'm considering losing weight to climb harder, read [[Morphology]] first — anthropometric factors explain a tiny fraction of grade variance, trainable factors explain most of it.
