# Hydration

Small effects on strength, larger on thermoregulation, one useful tactic on a hot day.

## Mechanism

Sweat evaporation is the main route for shedding core heat. Dehydration reduces sweating capacity, which interferes with evaporative loss, which raises core temperature, which further degrades performance. Self-reinforcing, which is why hydration matters most exactly when it's hardest to maintain.

There's also competition for blood flow — skin vasodilation to dump heat versus muscle perfusion. One proposed explanation for why finger flexors show lower endurance at higher temperatures. [[Temperature]].

## The tactic

Precooling works. Research on heat-stress-induced fatigue supports it, and the crag version is about a litre of a cold drink at ~4°C before or during a session. An ice slushy is better — the phase change absorbs more heat.

Real, cheap, and almost nobody does it. Freeze a bottle three-quarters full overnight, top with water in the morning, slushy at the boulder.

## Targets

- ~35 ml/kg/day baseline, more in heat. At 70 kg that's ~2.5 L.
- Don't hyperhydrate immediately pre-session. A full bladder and diluted electrolytes help nothing.
- 300-700 mg sodium per litre in hot conditions if I'm sweating heavily.
- Caffeine's diuretic effect is modest and overstated, not zero.

## The skin interaction, which cuts the other way

Systemic hydration good. Local fingertip moisture bad. Not in conflict — drinking water doesn't make my fingertips sweat more — but worth being clear that hydration-as-performance and dry-skin-as-friction are separate problems with separate solutions. [[Skin]].
