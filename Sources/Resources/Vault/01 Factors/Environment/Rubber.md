# Rubber

The one place I have real numbers, and then have to admit they explain less than they look like they do.

## Shore hardness

Vibram publishes XS Edge at 78 ± 3 Shore A, XS Grip 2 at 74 ± 3. Four points. A measurable difference, smaller than the marketing implies.

The pattern holds:

- Harder (XS Edge, firmer Stealth C4) resists deformation, so it holds a small edge with less foot strength and lasts longer on abrasive rock.
- Softer (XS Grip 2, Stealth HF) deforms into surface asperities, increasing real contact area, so it grips better on smooth rock and smears. Wears faster, especially on abrasive rock.

## What the marketing overstates

"Stickiness" as a single ranking. Rubber friction depends on counter-surface, contact pressure, temperature, and loading rate. A compound that wins on smooth limestone can lose on rough sandstone, because on rough rock I want a compound that resists being cut rather than one that flows into texture already there.

Also: midsole and last shape contribute more to how a shoe edges than four Shore points between compounds. If I change shoes and edging improves, it probably wasn't the rubber.

## Selection heuristic

| Rock and terrain | Bias |
| --- | --- |
| Small edges, vertical to slightly overhanging | Harder — XS Edge and equivalents |
| Smears, volumes, steep with poor feet | Softer — XS Grip 2, Stealth HF |
| Rough sandstone or granite friction slabs | Harder, for durability; the rock provides texture |
| Smooth polished limestone | Softer, for contact area |
| Cold conditions | One step softer than I'd otherwise pick — cold stiffens rubber |

Last row matters and almost nobody applies it.

## Practical

Two pairs with different compounds if I can. Match to the crux, not the boulder. And brush the rubber — it picks up dust and old chalk, and a dirty sole is a low-friction sole. A wipe on a damp towel before a real attempt is standard among people who send things.
