# Wind

No research. Large, immediate, well-known effect. Wind restores the evaporative gradient at the skin and rock surface that high [[Humidity]] destroys, so a breeze turns a marginal day into a good one without the temperature or dew point changing.

## Mechanism

Evaporation from a surface depends on the vapour pressure gradient between surface and the air immediately above it. Still air lets a saturated boundary layer sit on my fingertips and on the rock. Wind strips it. Why a boulder in a breeze feels dry at a dew point where a sheltered boulder feels greasy.

## Tactics

- **Windy boulders on humid days.** Ridge lines, gaps, exposed blocks. Sheltered forest boulders are the worst humid-day option.
- **Bring a fan.** Battery fans at boulder problems look ridiculous and work. Point it at the crux holds between attempts and at my hands during rests.
- **Read the terrain.** Wind funnels. A boulder at the mouth of a gully catches everything; one twenty metres inside catches nothing.
- **Time of day.** Thermal winds often build mid-morning and drop at dusk, which can beat the temperature drop for net conditions.

## Trade-off

Wind cools my hands, good for sweat and bad past a point. Cold fingers lose sensation and force. If I can't feel the crimp's edge the friction gain is irrelevant. Warm hands actively between attempts on cold windy days — pockets, hand warmers, movement — and accept the sweat cost.
