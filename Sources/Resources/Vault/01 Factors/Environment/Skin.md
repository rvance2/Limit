# Skin

My interface with the rock, my weak point, and the most manageable thing in [[Environment MOC]]. Historically sweaty, already on Antihydral, so this is about doing that well rather than deciding whether to.

## Narrow optimum

Skin needs enough moisture and pliability to conform to the rock's asperities, and not so much that a fluid layer forms. I'm on the wet side — the easier side to fix and the one with the worse failure mode, because the treatments overshoot and produce splits.

## Antihydral specifics

Mechanism: methenamine, which releases formaldehyde and denatures proteins in the sweat gland ducts, blocking sweat production. Delayed onset, long duration.

Practitioner consensus on timing:

- Apply 2-3 days before I need the effect. Not a same-day tool.
- Effects last roughly a week from one application, with wide individual variation.
- Repeated applications stack. Overshoot gives glassy, brittle skin that splits.
- A commonly cited high-end regime is every other night in the week before a trip, then every few days as conditions dictate. That's from a climber operating at 5.15 and it's not a starting point.
- Off the finger creases. Repeated application there causes splitting.
- Moisturise while using it. Skin doesn't heal without moisture and Antihydral removes it. This is the step I'd skip and it's why I'd split.

Skin types respond very differently. Track it. [[Skin Programme]].

## Healing timelines that constrain the plan

- A split takes up to a full week.
- A flapper needs immediate management then rest until healed.
- Muscles recover faster than skin. Climbed hard Monday → muscles ready Wednesday, skin Thursday.

Which is why skin belongs in a training plan and not just a first-aid kit. It's the binding constraint on attempt count during projecting, so it directly limits [[Patience]] and the value of any session.

## Daily routine

- After every session: wash, moisturise, lightly sand rough callus edges.
- Before bed: generous moisturiser. Cotton gloves overnight when chasing recovery hard.
- Hydrated skin regenerates faster than dry skin, which is the tension at the centre of managing sweaty hands — dry it for friction, moisturise it for recovery, on different clocks.
