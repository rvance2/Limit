# Calluses

Thickened epidermis including the dead outer layer, formed in response to repeated friction. Useful up to a point, then actively dangerous.

## Trade-off

A smooth, moderate callus spreads load and resists abrasion. A thick, ridged callus reduces sensitivity so I can't feel the best part of the hold, and creates an edge that catches and rips off in a large piece — a flapper and a week off sharp holds.

Goal isn't maximum callus. It's a flat, even, moderately thick surface with no proud edges.

## Maintenance

- **File, don't let it build.** Sandpaper or emery board after sessions, taking down rough edges and high spots until the fingertip is smooth. Two-minute job preventing a one-week problem.
- **Aim for flat.** Specifically remove the transition ridge at the callus edge. That ridge is what catches.
- **Don't sand to raw.** Removing the callus entirely leaves tender skin and no protection.
- **Grit progression.** ~120 for reducing thickness, 220-400 for finishing. Finish smooth or I've created new micro-edges.

## Timing

File the evening after a session, not before. Freshly filed skin is thinner and more sensitive, and a hard session on it goes through the layer. Filing 24-48 h before a project day lets it settle.

## Antihydral interaction

Antihydral-dried skin files differently — harder, more brittle, comes off in flakes rather than dust. Two consequences: easy to over-file, and dried callus cracks more readily at the edges. Another argument for keeping it flat and moisturised. [[Skin]].

## Splits

Form where callus meets soft skin under tension, usually in a crease or at a callus edge. Prevention is flat calluses plus moisture. Treatment is cleaning, liquid bandage or superglue for small ones, tape, and time. Nothing accelerates epidermal regrowth much.
