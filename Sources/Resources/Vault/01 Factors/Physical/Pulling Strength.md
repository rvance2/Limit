# Pulling Strength

Second-best physical predictor of bouldering grade, decays out of relevance faster than [[Finger Strength]], and the thing I've decided to chase anyway. Both facts stay in this note.

## The data

Lattice, n=901, 2RM weighted pull-up as % bodyweight, shoulder-width pronated, near-full extension to chin over bar:

| Group | R | adj R² | p |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Combined | 0.582 | 0.337 | <0.001 |
| Advanced | 0.346 | 0.117 | <0.0001 |
| Elite | 0.219 | 0.046 | <0.001 |
| Higher Elite | — | 0.009 | 0.164, ns |

At V13+ it explains under 1% and fails significance. At my level ~12%.

## Two separate goals, and I should stop conflating them

**Goal A: stop being pull-limited.** Probably already satisfied. 14 pull-ups on an average day, 23 on a strong one, clean muscle-up. [[Benchmarks]] puts the threshold around a 2RM with 30-40% bodyweight added and I may well be past it — though high-rep endurance and max strength diverge, so test rather than assume in [[Block 0 — Baseline Week]].

My own "sort of weak" self-assessment is probably relative to my one-arm goals rather than to the population. Worth knowing which.

**Goal B: one-arm pull-up and a long one-arm lock-off.** This is not a climbing performance goal. Plenty of climbers operating well above my grade cannot do a one-arm pull-up. If my V-grade doesn't move because I spent Tuesday hours here, that's the trade I chose, not a surprise.

I want it anyway. Fine. But the note says it plainly so I can't later be confused about why.

## The honest cost

Adding [[S6 Skill and Pull]] takes hours from [[Style Roulette]] and [[Movement Efficiency Drills]]. Per [[Factors MOC]] I put skill at ~25% of my gap and physical at ~40%, with finger strength the dominant share of that 40%. Pull strength is a slice of a slice.

So the pull work is capped: it never displaces [[Max Hangs]], [[MED Hangs]], [[Limit Bouldering]] or [[Shoulder Protocol]], and it lives on the one deliberately low-intensity day.

## Timeline

I've held a 10-second one-arm lock-off before and lost some over a vacation. That's stage 3 of [[One Arm Progression]] already achieved, and lock-off strength is heavily neural, so it returns in weeks.

Revised estimate: stages 4-6 across Blocks 2 and 3, stage 7 by Block 4 or shortly after, and a one-arm pull-up plausibly within six to twelve months. Not the 12-24 I first wrote, which assumed I was starting from nothing.

Still not chasing stage 8 inside [[Block 4 — Peak and Send]] — a maximal unilateral attempt inside a send window is a bad trade, and that reason has nothing to do with my shoulder.

## What my shoulder actually needs

Not restriction. [[Shoulder Capacity]] has the full correction: painless clicking in loaded underclings and cross-body isolations, nothing on bars or edges, never assessed. Probably a cuff-behind-prime-movers capacity gap rather than instability.

So the provocative positions become the training target in [[Deep Shoulder Positions]], and the bar work carries no shoulder-derived restrictions at all.
