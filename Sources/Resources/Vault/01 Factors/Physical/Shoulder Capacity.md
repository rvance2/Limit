# Shoulder Capacity

## What I actually have

Painless clicking, pinching, or a sense of the head sliding up or forwards, in two specific loaded positions:

1. **Thuggy underclings** — flexion plus external rotation, pulling up and out into the hold.
2. **Fully cross-body isolations**, especially with the other arm pulling the opposite direction — adduction and internal rotation across midline under rotational load.

No pain. No dead-arm. Never assessed. Bars and edges have never been an issue: 14 pull-ups on an average day, 23 on a strong one, muscle-up clean, and a 10-second one-arm lock-off before a vacation detrained some of it.

## What that isn't

It isn't the anterior instability pattern. Anterior instability is provoked by abduction plus external rotation — the arm-back position — and by the bottom of a dead hang. My bottom-of-pull-up position is fine at 23 reps. That rules the pattern out well enough to stop programming around it.

Nor is it a subluxation history. Subluxation is an event: a jolt, often a brief dead-arm, usually pain in the moment, and aching afterwards. Mine is positional, reproducible, painless, and leaves nothing behind. That's translation, not an event.

## What it probably is

The strong-climber pattern. Prime movers well ahead of the cuff. Lats, pecs and deltoid can generate more force than the rotator cuff can centre the humeral head against, so under high load in positions where the cuff is the limiter rather than the lats, the head translates a few millimetres and I feel it.

That story fits both provocative positions. A loaded undercling drives the head superiorly and anteriorly while the lats fire hard. A cross-body isolation with an opposing arm loads the posterior capsule and demands rotational control the cuff has to supply.

**This is a mechanism guess, not a diagnosis.** I have never had it assessed and I'm not qualified to. It fits, which is different from being true.

## What follows for training

Avoidance is the wrong move. If the cuff is the limiter, avoiding the positions that expose it guarantees the gap stays. So the provocative positions become the **training target**, loaded progressively. That's [[Deep Shoulder Positions]], and it doubles as the thing I actually want — deep shouldery moves being easier rather than avoided.

[[Shoulder Protocol]] gets rebuilt around this too: real load on the cuff at end range, not fifteen reps with a red band.

## Get it looked at, but don't wait for it

Book an assessment. Painless clicking is common and often benign, and I'm not in a position to know which kind mine is. But there's no reason to gate 22 weeks of training behind an appointment for a painless, positional, functionally silent sensation.

**Escalate to an assessment before continuing if any of these appear:**

- Pain, in the position or afterwards
- A dead-arm or sudden-weakness sensation
- A discrete event where it goes and comes back
- Symptoms spreading to bar or edge work, which has always been clean
- Night pain, numbness, or tingling down the arm

Those are the red flags. The clicking on its own is not one.

## What I got wrong before

The first version of this plan assumed anterior instability and built restrictions on top: bottom-of-range limits on pull-ups, a 2:1 row ratio, a six-week gate on unilateral work, a 90% load cap, dynos excluded. All of that was an inference from two words, and the inference was wrong. It's removed. [[Constraints MOC]] has the current list, which is much shorter and points at my actual positions.
