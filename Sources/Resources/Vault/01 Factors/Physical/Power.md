# Power

Force × velocity. On a limit boulder: move my centre of mass a long way, fast, from a bad position, and arrive somewhere I can hold.

Climbing has two established ways to train it — limit bouldering and campusing. No campus board, so limit bouldering carries the block. Not a downgrade. Limit bouldering trains power inside real movement so the coordination transfers; campusing trains a narrower thing harder.

## The data

The campus board frequency trial is the only decent intervention study. Sixteen advanced-to-elite climbers, 7a+ minimum, five weeks, volume matched across two vs four weekly sessions. Two/week produced the bouldering performance improvement (moves on 7A problems, moves-to-failure). Four/week produced RFD and maximal force. Authors recommended block periodisation for fatigue.

The awkward part: the frequency that built the quality wasn't the frequency that built the performance. My reading — four explosive sessions a week leaves me unable to climb well, and climbing well is what makes me better at climbing. Two dedicated power sessions.

## Energy reality

Power runs on phosphocreatine. PCr resynthesis largely complete 1-2 min, functionally complete 3-5. At 60 s rest I'm at ~85% of capacity, so attempt two is an 85% attempt. On a limit boulder an 85% attempt is wasted and it costs skin.

Most ignored fact in bouldering. Three to five minutes between limit attempts. Timer. See [[Patience]] and [[Energy]].

## Programming

- 2 sessions/week max in [[Block 3 — Power and RFD]].
- First thing after a thorough warm-up.
- 6-10 quality attempts total across 2-3 problems. Not 30.
- Never after a finger block at full intensity on the same day.
