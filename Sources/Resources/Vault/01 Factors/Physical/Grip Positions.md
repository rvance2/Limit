# Grip Positions

Same forearm, different force at different joint angles, different fatigue, different structures loaded. A real decision, not a detail.

## The data

Lattice analysed 460,000 prescribed fingerboard logs: 98% half crimp or plain edge, 1.1% pinch, under 1% open hand + slopers + pockets combined. Not evidence half crimp is best — evidence of what one large coaching operation prescribes.

Their assessment data is more interesting. 3,484 tested climbers, 71% half crimp, 29% open hand. At V1-V4 open-hand testers loaded higher (+22.5 kg vs +14.5 kg). By V12+ the two converged to within 0.2 kg (+55.5 vs +55.3). Strong climbers end up equally strong in both. Whether half-crimp training causes that or accompanies it isn't established.

The peer-reviewed literature disagrees with itself. Some studies find half crimp produces more force independent of skill. A recent *CISS* paper found the opposite — slightly higher force open hand, all skill levels, both sexes. Two careful studies pointing opposite ways means the difference is small and swamped by individual variation and edge geometry.

One finding that holds: force correlates with skill, experience, and training frequency in *both* positions. Grip strength tracks training, not grip choice.

## Structural cost

Full crimp raises pulley forces. Well established, and why it's late and small in my plan.

Half crimp loads the forearm most broadly — doesn't hammer the DIP joints like full crimp, doesn't bias the long flexors over the intrinsics like a deep open hand. Whether that makes it the ideal base grip or a defensible one is a judgement call. Lattice make the strong version and I find it convincing without thinking it's proven.

## My decision

Block 1: half crimp on 20 mm only. One position, five weeks.

Block 2 onward: [[Grip Specificity Block]] enters — front-three drag plus one position dictated by what the project actually demands. Moved earlier than version one had it, because the convergence data above says breadth is a base property and because Block 3 now belongs to rate work.

Full crimp: yielding sets only, peak block, before climbing not after, only if the project needs it. [[Tendon Health]].

Nelson's useful note — 20 mm is about right for full crimp, 25-30 mm is better for loading connective tissue, since larger edges up to ~30 mm allow higher peak vertical force.
