# Finger Strength

Peak isometric force through the finger flexors. Everything else in [[Physical MOC]] is downstream of it or competing with it for session time.

## The data

Lattice, n=901, 7-second two-arm max hang on 20 mm half crimp, against best boulder worked over two years:

| Group | R | adj R² | p |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Combined | 0.704 | 0.496 | <0.001 |
| Advanced (V4-V8) | 0.411 | 0.167 | <0.0001 |
| Elite (V9-V12) | 0.319 | 0.099 | <0.001 |
| Higher Elite (V13+) | 0.225 | 0.042 | 0.019 |

The combined figure is inflated by range — pooling V1 with V15 manufactures correlation. Within-group numbers are the honest ones.

I'm V8-V10, straddling the Advanced/Elite boundary, so the share finger strength explains for me sits somewhere between 17% and 10% and is falling as I move up. Largest single share of anything they tested. Still the minority of the picture, and shrinking. [[Factors MOC]].

Stien et al. (2021, n=57: 17 intermediate, 25 advanced, 15 elite), maximal force in an isometric pull-up on 23 mm at 90° elbow. Elite above advanced (ES 1.78, p<0.001) and intermediate (ES 1.77, p<0.001). Intermediate vs advanced: no difference (p=0.898).

Force isn't a smooth ramp across ability. Flat, then it jumps. A secondary summary gives group means around 2519 N elite, 1272 advanced, 1166 intermediate — those live in a figure image, unverified. Effect sizes above are from the abstract and are solid. See [[Sources]].

## Benchmarks

Lattice, male, 7 s two-arm on 20 mm, % bodyweight including bodyweight: V4 128%, V7 146%, V11 170%. Interpolates at ~6% per grade. One-arm pull on 20 mm: V14 106%, V15 110%, V16 114%, V17 118% — linear 4% per grade.

Everything between V11 and V14 in the circulated table is a blogger's extrapolation. My band on the two-arm table runs roughly V8 = 152% to V10 = 164% of bodyweight — about 12% of bodyweight to cross it. [[Benchmarks]].

## Training it

Two families, not interchangeable.

Yielding — hang and resist. [[Max Hangs]], [[MED Hangs]]. Highest specificity to how a hold loads me, easiest to overload precisely, hardest on tissue.

Overcoming — pull against something immovable. [[Recruitment Pulls]]. Nelson reports ~30% force difference between the two, direction consistent with the concentric-eccentric asymmetry. His claim is that overcoming drives recruitment while heavy hangs bias toward tissue stiffness. Mechanism argument, no climbing trial. So I run both rather than betting.

López's eight-week comparison in 7c+/8a climbers: MaxHangs with added weight on 18 mm for four weeks, then minimum-edge MaxHangs for four, produced the maximal-strength result. Intermittent hangs biased endurance. That sequencing is the backbone of [[Block 1 — Base]] into [[Block 2 — Max Strength]].

## Ceiling

Never at the end of a session. Fingerboard, campus and limit bouldering go in the first half, nervous system fresh. And hold one or two methods for 6-12 weeks before judging them. Method-hopping every three weeks is how I'd train fingers for years without getting stronger fingers.
