# Tendon Health

The rate limiter on everything in [[Physical MOC]]. Muscle adapts in weeks; tendon, pulley and capsule in months. Programmes fail when the strength curve outruns the tissue curve.

## What loads what

- Full crimp raises flexor pulley forces relative to half crimp and open hand. Established, and why full crimp is late and small here.
- Larger edges up to ~30 mm permit higher peak vertical force, making them the better choice when the goal is loading connective tissue rather than reaching a minimum edge. Nelson: recruitment work first on a smaller edge, then 25-30 mm specifically for connective tissue.
- High-intensity fast-rate loading is what increases tendon stiffness. Slow submaximal work does something else.

## Nutrition timing

15 g gelatin or hydrolysed collagen with ~50 mg vitamin C, 30-60 min before loading, roughly doubles serum PINP in Baar and Shaw's work. Amino acids peak in serum around 60 min post-ingestion, which is why timing matters and why taking it after the session is close to pointless. A 14-week trial of specific collagen peptides alongside high-load resistance training found increased patellar tendon cross-sectional area.

One of the better-evidenced supplements in sport and one of the cheapest. Not magic — the loading does the work, the collagen supplies substrate. [[Tendon Nutrition Timing]].

## Warning signs, in order

1. Morning stiffness in a finger, clears within a few minutes of moving. Monitor.
2. Stiffness taking over 15 min to clear, or worse than yesterday. Cut finger load 30%, hold a week.
3. Localised tenderness on palpation over a pulley or joint. Stop small-edge and crimp work. Half crimp on 25-30 mm only.
4. Pop, click, or sudden sting during a catch or crimp. Session over. Physio.

Four is the one I'll rationalise. Don't.

## If a pulley does go

Not a plan, a structure — from a documented case in [[Burden of Dreams — Wheeler and Shriber]] where an athlete ruptured a pulley, worked with a PT, and returned stronger. Tier 3, n=1.

- **Keep loading everything else immediately.** Slab, feet-only, toe work, one-arm climbing for coordination, continued lifting. The goal is not losing general fitness while the finger heals.
- **Graded progressive overload on the injured hand.** 15-second pulls on a 20 mm edge, starting light and progressing over roughly a month, with **parity against the uninjured hand** as the target rather than an absolute number.
- **Pulley ring for support** once climbing resumes.
- Expect the *uninjured* fingers to feel better afterwards. Forced rest does that.

**What not to copy:** he climbed two days after the rupture on a peer's advice. Elite athlete, professional oversight, competition deadline. That is not general advice and I shouldn't read it as permission.

The actual first move if this happens is a physio, not a protocol off the internet.

## Progression ceiling

No more than 2.5-5% added weight per session on [[Max Hangs]]. No more than one edge step per two weeks on [[MED Hangs]]. If I can add more than that, I wasn't at the prescribed effort level last session.
