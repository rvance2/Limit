# Height and Span

Split from [[Morphology]] because it gets blamed more than anything else on the list.

Two conventions and they get mixed up. Ratio — span ÷ height — is dimensionless, neutral at 1.0. Difference — span − height in cm — is neutral at 0. "+2 ape index" means nothing until I say which.

## Summary

Height and span don't predict grade. They predict which beta is available.

Long span helps on reachy moves and hurts in compressed, scrunched positions where I can't get my hips in. Short span is the reverse. Neither is an advantage overall, which is what the systematic review shows: no significant difference in ape index across skill levels.

Real cost of long limbs: leverage. Longer forearm is a longer moment arm at the elbow; longer torso is more mass further from the wall. [[Leverage]]. Real benefit: reach past a blank section, skip an intermediate.

## What to do instead of complaining

1. Measure the reach requirement. Chalk the target, chalk my max reach from the stance, compare. Most "too tall for me" cruxes are two inches short of impossible and fixable with a hip position.
2. Build the short-person beta deliberately. Usually an intermediate, a different foot, or a bump. Nearly always slower and more secure.
3. Build the tall-person beta if I'm the tall one — usually a static reach that skips a dynamic move. Tall climbers who climb like short climbers waste the advantage.
