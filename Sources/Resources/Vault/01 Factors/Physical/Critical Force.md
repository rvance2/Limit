# Critical Force

Force my finger flexors can sustain indefinitely, plus the impulse above it. Fryer/Giles protocol: rhythmic all-out isometrics, 7 s on / 3 s off, half crimp on 20 mm into a force sensor, 4-5 min until output plateaus. CF = mean end-test force. W′ = impulse above it.

Good measure. Wrong goal.

## Why it doesn't matter here

Lattice, 901 climbers, 60%-of-max repeaters to exhaustion vs best boulder worked: R = 0.007, adj R² = −0.001, p = 0.830. Nothing, at every ability level.

My boulder is four moves long. Force production after ninety seconds doesn't enter it.

## Why I keep the note

1. If the boulder has a long tenuous topout, or I go back to sport climbing, CF is what gets me to the lip.
2. It's a good recovery marker. A CF test 8% below baseline is objective evidence I'm accumulating fatigue faster than clearing it. Monitoring tool, not target. [[Readiness Monitoring]].
3. Base-phase capacity work is protective for tissue tolerance even where it doesn't raise the ceiling.

## Programming

Minimal. [[Repeaters]] twice weekly in [[Block 1 — Base]] at low intensity, gone after week 6. With a load cell: one CF test at baseline, one at week 11 as a fatigue check. Otherwise skip.
