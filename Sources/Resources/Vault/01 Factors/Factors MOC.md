# Factors MOC

Thirty-odd things decide whether a limit boulder goes. Not equal, and their weights shift as I get better.

## Where I sit

V8-V10. That straddles a real boundary in the data: Lattice group Advanced as V4-V8 and Elite as V9-V12, and the predictors change across it.

## The two datasets that matter

**Lattice, n=901, bouldering.** Finger strength against best boulder worked, by band:

| Band | Finger adj R² | Pull adj R² |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Advanced (V4-V8) | 0.167 | 0.117 |
| Elite (V9-V12) | 0.099 | 0.046 |
| Higher Elite (V13+) | 0.042 | ns (p=0.164) |

Crossing from Advanced to Elite roughly halves what finger strength explains. It stays the largest single physical predictor. It stops being the whole story.

**Stien et al. 2022, n=78, sport climbing.** Isometric pull-up on a 23 mm rung, three IRCRA bands:

| Measure | Intermediate → Advanced | Advanced → Elite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Peak force | +40.1% (ES 1.24) | +39.7% (ES 1.40) |
| Average force | +59.1% (ES 1.57) | +45.6% (**ES 4.60**) |
| **Rate of force development** | +45.5% (ES 1.42) | **+74.9%** (ES 1.42) |

Peak force climbs at a near-constant ~40% per band. **RFD nearly doubles going into elite.** The authors' own reading: RFD becomes an increasingly important limiting factor once elite grades are reached, because those grades feature steeper terrain and longer moves that demand rapid force rather than sustained force.

Caveat: sport climbing, not bouldering. IRCRA 24-27 is roughly French 8a-8b+. The transfer to bouldering is plausible and assumed, not demonstrated.

## The methodological problem, stated plainly

All of this is cross-sectional. It compares V10 climbers to V12 climbers at one moment. It does not follow V10 climbers to see who becomes V12 and what they did. Cross-sectional designs cannot establish temporal precedence.

There is survivorship in it too. The V13+ group are people who got to V13. The ones who trained identically and plateaued or got injured aren't in the sample. So "elite climbers have X" is not evidence that acquiring X makes me elite.

Nobody has run the study I'd actually want. The gap between "what differs between bands" and "what moves you between bands" is where most coaching folklore lives, and I should hold everything below with that in mind.

## Domains

- [[Physical MOC]] — force, rate, tension, tissue. The part with numbers.
- [[Skill MOC]] — how much of that force reaches the rock.
- [[Mind MOC]] — whether I get the good try.
- [[Recovery MOC]] — whether any of it accumulates.
- [[Environment MOC]] — what I don't control and must forecast.

## My weighting, revised for V8-V10

| Domain | Share of my gap | Change | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Physical | 35% | ↓ from 40 | Finger strength's explanatory power halves across the band I'm crossing |
| Skill | 30% | ↑ from 25 | Takes up the slack as strength stops discriminating |
| Mind | 15% | — | Attempt discipline is still my specific failure mode. [[Patience]] |
| Recovery | 15% | — | Decides whether the other 85% compounds |
| Environment | 5% | — | Small across a season, decisive on the day |

My judgement, not a measurement. It exists so the plan has a defensible allocation of hours.

## The bigger change is *inside* Physical

The share barely moved. The composition moved a lot.

- **Peak force** stays the base and stays the largest single predictor. It doesn't stop mattering.
- **[[Rate of Force Development]] moves up hard.** It's what widens between the band I'm in and the band above. [[Block 3 — Power and RFD]] extended from four weeks to six for this reason.
- **[[Grip Positions]] breadth moves earlier.** Lattice: at V1-V4 open-hand testers loaded +22.5 kg against half crimp's +14.5 kg; by V12+ the two converge within 0.2 kg. The strongest climbers have no weak positions. [[Grip Specificity Block]] moved from Block 3 into Block 2.
- **[[Critical Force]] stays at zero.** R = 0.007 at every band. Nothing changed there.

## Don't drop the ceiling pusher

An allocation heuristic from [[Burden of Dreams — Wheeler and Shriber]], no data behind it, useful anyway.

A boulder demands a *combined* score of strength and technique, and the two are partly substitutable — being much stronger means needing less technique, because the move can be overpowered. So the quality I'm a super-adapter in is doing load-bearing work, and dropping it to zero while I train a weakness costs more than the weakness gains.

The coach's rule: keep it at one to two sessions a week during a block aimed elsewhere. Maintenance, not development.

For me that means the pull and power work doesn't disappear during [[Block 2 — Max Strength]], and the finger work doesn't disappear during [[Block 3 — Power and RFD]]. Both already run at maintenance in those blocks, which is the same idea arrived at from the periodisation side.

## Untrainable

[[Height and Span]], [[Morphology]], [[Rock]], [[Wind]]. Trainable variables explain roughly 59% of climbing performance variance against about 0.3% for anthropometrics. Whatever limits me, it isn't my proportions. [[Morphology]].
