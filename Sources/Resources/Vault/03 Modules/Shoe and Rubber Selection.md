# Shoe and Rubber Selection

A week-19 task, not an ongoing one. Pick for the crux, not the boulder.

## Decision

| Crux demand | Choose |
| --- | --- |
| Small flat edges, vertical to slightly steep | Stiff midsole, harder rubber — XS Edge (78 ± 3 Shore A) or equivalent |
| Smears, volumes, steep with poor feet | Soft midsole, softer rubber — XS Grip 2 (74 ± 3) or Stealth HF |
| Decisive heel hook | Best heel fit I own, whatever the compound |
| Toe hook | Best toe patch and a snug forefoot |
| Rough sandstone or granite | Harder, for durability. The rock supplies texture. |
| Polished limestone | Softer, for real contact area |
| Cold conditions | One step softer than I'd otherwise pick — cold stiffens rubber |

Last row is real and almost nobody applies it.

## Fit checks that outrank the model

- **No dead space in front of the big toe.** Dead space means the shoe deforms before force reaches the rock. Largest single edging loss.
- **Heel volume matched.** A loose heel rolls in the cup and heel hooks slip.
- **Upper not stretched.** The failure I'd miss. A stretched upper stops transmitting force even with good rubber, because the foot moves inside it. Usually happens before the rubber wears out, and it's why a resoled shoe "never feels the same." It was the upper.

## Retirement

Either trigger:

1. Midsole visible at the toe. Edging gone.
2. Upper stretched enough that my foot shifts under load. Test: stand on a small edge, see whether the shoe rolls before the foot does.

## Project day

- Fresh pair, not broken-in. Break them in during weeks 19-20 on easy terrain, then save them.
- Shoes inside my jacket between attempts in cold conditions, on warm. Cold rubber stiffens and loses conformity. Free performance.
- Wipe the soles on a damp cloth before every real attempt. Rubber picks up dust and old chalk; a dirty sole is a low-friction sole.

## What the marketing overstates

"Stickiness" as a single ranking. Rubber friction depends on counter-surface, contact pressure, temperature and loading rate. A compound that wins on smooth limestone can lose on rough sandstone. And midsole plus last shape contribute more to edging than four Shore points between compounds — if I change shoes and edging improves, it probably wasn't the rubber.
