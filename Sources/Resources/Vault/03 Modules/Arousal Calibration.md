# Arousal Calibration

Find my zone by measuring it, because my memory of my best performances is unreliable.

## The model

Hanin's Individual Zones of Optimal Functioning. Optimal activation is individual, and the population-average inverted-U says nothing about me. A meta-analysis of 19 studies, 146 effect sizes, 6,387 participants gave reasonable support to the in-zone versus out-of-zone distinction.

Also: helpful versus unhelpful, not positive versus negative. Anger can be functional. Calm can be dysfunctional.

## Protocol

Before every attempt on a limit boulder or project, log two numbers and one word. Five seconds.

```
Attempt 4 | arousal 7 | cue "left hip" | crux hold, both hands, fell on the move to the lip
```

Arousal 1-10, where 1 is nearly asleep and 10 is shaking. After 30-40 logged attempts, look at where my successes and best attempts cluster.

## What I'll probably find

Common pattern in boulderers: best attempts at 6-7, not 9-10, because 9-10 costs precision, and [[Precision]] degrades at high arousal before strength does. Why the fourth try, frustrated and pulling harder, is often less accurate than the second.

A pattern, not my number. The point is finding mine.

## Task-dependence

Not one number. Two:

- Powerful crux needing maximum force: probably higher than I think.
- Technical crux needing a delicate foot: lower.

Log which type each attempt was so I can separate them.

## Manipulating it

**Up:** music, short explosive movement, breath-holds, competitive framing, a near-limit warm-up boulder immediately before. Potentiation from a near-maximal effort raises output for a window measured in minutes, which is why sitting in the shade for twenty minutes after the good warm-up boulder wastes it. [[Recruitment]].

**Down:** slow nasal breathing with a long exhale, deliberately slowing movement between attempts, extending the rest, shoes off.

## Pre-attempt routine

Four beats, identical every time, under fifteen seconds: chalk, breath, cue, pull on. A fixed routine removes decisions, which frees attention. Longer and it's a ritual.
