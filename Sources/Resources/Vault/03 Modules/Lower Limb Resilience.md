# Lower Limb Resilience

I get extreme shin splints rarely. Rarely is enough to plan around, because the trigger for a boulderer is almost always an approach — a sudden block of steep hiking with a heavy pad, on legs that have done nothing but stand on footholds for three months.

## What MTSS is

Medial tibial stress syndrome is understood primarily as a bone overload condition — repetitive axial loading producing microtrauma and periosteal irritation — rather than a soft-tissue problem. That reframing matters: the treatment is load management, not stretching.

Management consensus: modify the aggravating activity, correct biomechanical contributors, structured strengthening, gradual monitored return. No single intervention is a cure. Compression, shock-absorbing insoles and shockwave therapy have some support as adjuncts.

## Prophylactic session

Twice weekly, 8 min, end of session.

1. **Heel raises, straight knee.** 3 × 15, slow, full range, off a step. Gastrocnemius.
2. **Heel raises, bent knee.** 3 × 15. Soleus — the one that matters for tibial loading and the one everyone skips.
3. **Tibialis anterior raises.** 3 × 20. Toes up against a wall or with a band. Loads the anterior compartment directly.
4. **Single-leg balance, eyes closed.** 3 × 30 s each side.
5. **Ankle dorsiflexion mobilisation.** Shared with [[Hip and Ankle Mobility]].

## Approach load management

The rule that prevents the problem: don't let approach volume jump. No pad hiking in six weeks then three consecutive days of steep approaches is the spike that produces the flare.

- Two weeks before a trip with real approaches, add two 30-45 min walks per week with a weighted pack.
- Weekly walking volume up no more than 20-30%.
- On multi-day trips, alternate a long-approach day with a roadside day.

## If a flare starts

1. Reduce impact immediately. Total rest isn't required; changing the loading is.
2. Keep climbing. Standing on footholds isn't axial impact loading.
3. Cut walking and running to pain-free volume, rebuild ~10% per week.
4. Continue items 1-3 above at pain-free load.
5. Not settling in three weeks, or pain becoming focal on the tibia rather than diffuse along it → imaging. Focal bone pain is a stress fracture question.
