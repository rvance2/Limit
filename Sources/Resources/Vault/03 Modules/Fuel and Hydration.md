# Fuel and Hydration

## Daily frame

| When | What |
| --- | --- |
| 60 min pre | 15 g collagen or gelatin + ~50 mg vitamin C. [[Tendon Nutrition Timing]] |
| 2-3 h pre | 1-1.5 g/kg carbohydrate, moderate protein, low fibre |
| Sessions over 90 min | 30-60 g/h carbohydrate |
| Within 2 h post | ~0.3 g/kg protein, carbohydrate to appetite |
| Daily | ~35 ml/kg fluid baseline, more in heat |

## Carbohydrate isn't optional

High-intensity work runs on carbohydrate, and impaired carbohydrate utilisation is a documented consequence of chronic under-fuelling in the RED-S literature. No fasted or low-carb limit bouldering. I'd produce less force, learn less, recover worse.

## Precooling, which almost nobody does

Research on heat-stress-induced fatigue supports precooling to prolong heat tolerance. At a crag: about a litre at ~4°C before or during a hot session. A slushy is better — phase change absorbs more heat.

Freeze a bottle three-quarters full overnight, top with water in the morning. Real, cheap intervention for summer bouldering. [[Temperature]] and [[Hydration]].

## Sodium

300-700 mg per litre in hot conditions if I'm sweating heavily. Dehydration reduces sweating capacity → less evaporative cooling → higher core temperature → worse performance. The loop matters most when hydration is hardest to maintain.

## Caffeine

The only climbing-specific RCT gave 3 mg/kg to intermediate-advanced climbers and found no statistically or practically meaningful effect on pull-up or grip performance. Participants reported significantly greater subjective strength, energy and alertness.

Use it for alertness on an early project day if feeling capable helps. No performance increment budgeted. Nothing after midday if it costs sleep.

## RED-S

Read [[Energy]] before any deliberate weight loss. Documented consequences of low energy availability: reduced bone mineral density and microstructure, endocrine disruption, impaired carbohydrate use, reduced performance. Climbing selects for low bodyweight and rewards it short-term, which makes it structurally prone to this.

Anthropometric factors explain a tiny fraction of grade variance. Trainable factors explain most of it. [[Morphology]].
