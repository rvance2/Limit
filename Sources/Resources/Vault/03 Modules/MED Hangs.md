# MED Hangs

Minimum edge depth at bodyweight. Reduce the edge instead of adding weight. López's second maximal-strength method, and what follows [[Max Hangs]] in her sequencing.

## Dose

`3-5 x MED Hangs x 5"-12" (1-3) :3'-5'`

- Smallest edge I could hold for the target duration plus margin.
- Bodyweight only.
- 5-12 s.
- Margin 1-3 s.
- 3-5 min rest.

## Why it comes second

López's eight-week comparison in 7c+/8a climbers: the maximal-strength result came from four weeks of MAW Hangs on a relatively deep edge (18 mm) then four weeks of MED Hangs on small edges. Deep edge with added weight builds the force. Small edge at bodyweight teaches me to express it at short joint angles and on holds that resemble project crimps.

Don't invert. Small edges early in a block, before tissue has adapted to the block's load, is the standard route to a pulley problem.

## Equipment without a variable-depth board

No Progression or Transgression board with eight graded edges. Workarounds:

- Most hangboards have several depths. Measure with calipers, write the numbers on the board. Probably three or four usable steps.
- Wooden rungs are cheap. Loose rungs at 12, 15 and 18 mm covers 22 weeks.
- Failing that, [[Max Hangs]] with added weight and progress by load alone, accepting the loss of small-edge specificity.

## Progression

- One edge step down every two weeks maximum.
- Within an edge, +1 s per session to the top of the range, then step down.
- Twice weekly.

## Stop rules

Same as [[Max Hangs]], plus: on the smallest edge, any sense that fingertip skin is the limiting factor means stop. That's testing skin, not fingers, and I pay in [[Skin]] for no strength gain.
