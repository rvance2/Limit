# Beta Lab

A procedure for not committing to the sequence I invented while I knew least about the boulder.

## The problem

I converge on a sequence within two or three attempts, then spend fifteen sessions trying to get strong enough for it. Coaching observation rather than a research finding, and the single most likely cause of a stalled V8.

## Protocol

**Session 1 on any new project: no full attempts.**

1. Work the boulder in sections, top down. Learn the moves in isolation.
2. Per crux, write three sequences before trying any properly. In the note, in words, not memory.
3. One must assume a body shorter than mine. One must assume more flexibility than I have. Forces me off the default.
4. Try each at least twice.
5. Score each: possible / possible on a good day / not possible.

**Then commit.** Choose one and give it three sessions. Don't re-open the question inside those three. Beta-hopping and method-hopping are the same failure and both feel like progress.

**Vector experiments before strength conclusions.** Before concluding a move needs more strength, run five: rotate the hip, drop the shoulder, change which foot is loaded, move a foot two centimetres, change the weighting order. Most "too hard" moves were the wrong angle. [[Leverage]].

## After choosing: build the sims

Once a sequence is chosen, [[Simulation Sets]] turns it into four or five gym problems that each approximate the crux differently. That's the answer to having no systems board, and it's insurance against the exact failure this module exists to prevent — committing to one guess at the crux.

## Filming

Two angles if possible, side-on and three-quarter. Side-on shows the hip path, where [[Movement Efficiency]] lives. Three-quarter shows hand positions. Watch it that evening, not at the crag.

## Morphology honesty

Before blaming my height, measure the reach requirement. Chalk the target, chalk my maximum reach from the stance, compare. Most "too tall for me" cruxes are two inches short of impossible. [[Height and Span]].
