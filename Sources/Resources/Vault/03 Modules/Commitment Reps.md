# Commitment Reps

Graded exposure to a specific move I won't go for. The subject is the decision, not the movement.

## Resource problem, not courage problem

On a bad crimp or poor foot, holding the position costs force. Deliberating there spends the force the move needed. Committing early often succeeds on strength the hesitant version also had and spent on thinking.

More useful than the moralising version, and it points at the fix: make the decision before I'm in the position.

## Protocol

1. **Identify the specific move.** Not "I'm not committing." The exact move, the exact instant I back off.
2. **Make the consequence trivially safe.** More pads, a spotter, the move lower on the wall, two feet off the ground.
3. **Execute at full commitment ten times in the safe version.** Ten. Reps of the *go*, not the mechanics.
4. **Restore one element of consequence.** Fewer pads, or higher, or no spot. Ten more.
5. **Restore the real version.**

## The falling piece

MacLeod: fear of falling is the largest single impediment to climbing performance and physical training doesn't touch it. His approach is to address the specific impulses creating the fear while staying oriented to the satisfaction of doing the move, rather than fighting fear head-on. Systematic, not heroic.

On boulders the fear is smaller than on lead but not zero — the bad landing, the fall where a foot might catch, the heel hook I won't weight because of how I'd come off. Specific, and each separately addressable.

## Practice falls

If a specific fall is the block, practise the fall. Deliberately. Ten times from the position, onto the pads, with a spotter. Not the move — the fall. I've never fallen from the crux on purpose, so my body has no data about it and defaults to protection.

## What doesn't work

Talking myself into it in the moment. By then the decision needs to be made. Deciding on the wall *is* hesitation.
