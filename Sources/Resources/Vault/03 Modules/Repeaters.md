# Repeaters

Intermittent dead hangs. Here for tissue tolerance in the base block, then gone, because the data says they don't predict what I care about.

## Honest framing

Lattice's 60%-of-max repeaters-to-exhaustion test correlated with best boulder worked at R = 0.007 across 901 climbers. Not significant at any ability level. For a 30-move sport route this module would carry a block. My goal is four moves, so it gets six weeks of low-intensity tissue work and leaves.

## Dose

López's IntHangs notation: `3-4 x 4-5 x MED IntHangs x 7"-10" :20"/2'`

- 3-4 sets of 4-5 reps.
- 7-10 s hangs, 20 s between reps, 2 min between sets.
- Edge chosen so I reach failure on the last rep of the last set, not before.
- Half crimp, 20 mm or larger.
- Once weekly in [[Block 1 — Base]] only, weeks 1-5.

No margin here, unlike [[Max Hangs]]. IntHangs are designed to reach failure at the end. Failing too early or not at all produces a different effect than intended, so I adjust edge or weight between sets to land it.

## Why keep it

Three reasons, none of them power endurance.

1. Tissue tolerance. Accumulated time under submaximal load appears to build tendon capacity, raising the ceiling for the heavy blocks that follow.
2. Grip position practice under moderate load, where a sloppy half crimp gets fixed cheaply.
3. A baseline for [[Critical Force]] as a fatigue marker if I have a load cell. A CF test 8% below baseline in week 12 is real information. [[Readiness Monitoring]].

## Stop rules

- Finger pain as opposed to fatigue. Fatigue burns and spreads. Pain is sharp and local.
- If this compromises [[Max Hangs]] two days later, cut it. Lowest-priority finger work in the plan.
