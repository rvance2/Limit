# Simulation Sets

Four or five gym problems that each approximate my project's crux in a slightly different way. Train across all of them rather than building one replica.

Source: [[Burden of Dreams — Wheeler and Shriber]]. Tier 3 anecdote, and the most directly useful idea in it for me.

## Why this rather than a replica

I have no systems board and no way to reproduce specific holds. A single approximate replica would be a bad copy of one guess at the crux.

The shotgun version turns that limitation into an advantage:

1. **Beta uncertainty is covered.** I arrive prepared for several versions of the move rather than the one I guessed at home. Since [[Beta Lab]] says I converge on a sequence while knowing least about the boulder, this is insurance against my own worst habit.
2. **No pigeonholing.** Five adjacent problems make me better broadly. One replica makes me better at one replica.
3. **Adherence.** Five problems don't get stale the way one does.

## Building the set

Once a project is chosen in [[Beta Lab]], set four to five problems. Each isolates a different dimension of the crux, and each should be near my limit.

| Sim | Isolates | Built by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | The hold type | Same grip position, easier position |
| B | The body position | Same position, better holds |
| C | The move's force demand | Same span and angle, different holds |
| D | The tension requirement | Steeper, worse feet, similar hand sequence |
| E | The entry | The move *into* the crux, which is where I actually fail |

Sim E is the one I'd skip and shouldn't. [[Pacing and Rhythm]] says arriving at the crux with the hips slightly wrong is a setup problem that reads as a strength problem.

## Dose

- Rotate through the set inside [[Limit Bouldering]] sessions in Blocks 3 and 4.
- Two sims per session, not all five. Same attempt budget and same [[Attempt Discipline]] rules — the sims don't get their own extra attempts.
- Rebuild any sim that becomes easy. A sim I can do is no longer a sim.

## Reviewing it

Log which sim feels closest to the real thing after each outdoor session. That's the one to weight. The others get rebuilt or dropped.

If all five feel wrong after a day on the boulder, my model of the crux is wrong, which is information worth more than the training was.
