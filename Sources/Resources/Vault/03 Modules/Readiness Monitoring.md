# Readiness Monitoring

Sixty seconds every morning. Decides what today's session actually is.

## The morning five

```
Date | HRV | resting HR | sleep h | motivation 1-5 | any finger stiff on waking? Y/N
```

HRV needs a chest strap or a wearable with a standardised morning protocol. Without one, resting HR plus the subjective items. Cruder, still works.

## Decision rule

Count how many are true:

- HRV more than about 10% below my rolling baseline
- Sleep under 7 hours
- Resting HR up more than 5 bpm
- Motivation flat
- Any finger stiff on waking

| Count | Today |
| --- | --- |
| 0-1 | As prescribed |
| 2 | Downgrade: keep the session, cut top-end intensity 10-15%, drop the last set of everything |
| 3 | Skill and mobility only. [[Movement Efficiency Drills]], [[Footwork Precision]], [[Hip and Ankle Mobility]] |
| 4-5 | Rest. [[Visualization Protocol]] and [[Shoulder Protocol]] |

The rule exists so the decision gets made at 7am by a rule rather than at 6pm by my ego.

## Weekly load

Session RPE: rate 1-10, multiply by minutes. Sum for the week = acute. Four-week average = chronic. Ratio has a proposed safe zone of 0.8-1.3.

Honest about that number. ACWR has been criticised as methodologically flawed and misused, and the injury-prediction literature is mixed. Guardrail on the *direction* — a week far heavier than recent average is a risk — not a threshold with meaning at 1.3.

The monitoring literature favours multi-indicator surveillance over any single metric: HRV, session RPE, sleep, skill-specific load together. Which is what this is.

## Objective check, with a load cell

[[Critical Force]] test at baseline, week 12, week 19. A drop of 8% or more from baseline is real evidence I'm accumulating fatigue faster than I clear it, and more trustworthy than how I feel. Monitoring tool, not training target.

## The four fatigues

Different timescales, different responses. Know which one I have. [[Fatigue]]:

- Phosphagen, minutes: rest longer between attempts.
- Peripheral forearm, hours: end the finger work, other work may be fine.
- Neural, days: RFD drops before peak force and I feel fine. Ruins power sessions invisibly.
- Tissue, weeks: morning stiffness, palpation tenderness, longer warm-ups. Cut load 30% for a week.

Tissue is the one that ruins plans, because its timescale is longer than my attention span.
