# One Arm Progression

Toward a one-arm pull-up, via lock-off strength. I have done a 10-second one-arm lock-off before and lost some of it over a vacation, so this is re-acquisition, not building from nothing.

## Entry point

**Stage 3.** Not stage 0. A 10-second unassisted one-arm lock-off at full is already stage 3 on this ladder.

Re-test in [[Block 0 — Baseline Week]] to see where the detraining actually left me. One-arm lock-off is heavily neural, so what was lost over a vacation comes back in weeks rather than months. Expect the first four weeks to be steep and then to slow down.

If the re-test lands below stage 3, start where it lands. Don't start below where I test.

## Evidence status

No published protocol. "Hoseok Lee style" is reconstructed from what he posts publicly — heavy low-rep weighted pull-ups, high-frequency one-arm lock-offs at multiple joint angles, one-arm negatives. He is among the strongest pullers in climbing and it plainly works for him, which is not a sample size.

The prerequisite heuristic below is calisthenics lore with no research behind it. Tier 3. [[Sources]].

## The ladder

Both sides, every session, after warm-up.

| Stage | Exercise | Target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Two-arm lock-off at full, 90°, 120° | 3 × 20 s each angle |
| 1 | One-arm scapular hang, scapula set | 5 × 10 s each |
| 2 | One-arm lock-off at full, other hand assisting at the wrist | 5 × 8 s each |
| 3 | **One-arm lock-off at full, unassisted** ← entry | 3 × 10 s each |
| 4 | One-arm lock-off at 90° elbow | 3 × 8 s each |
| 5 | One-arm lock-off at 120° elbow | 3 × 5 s each |
| 6 | One-arm negative from full lock, 5 s descent | 3 × 3 each |
| 7 | Band or pulley-assisted one-arm pull-up, assistance reducing | 3 × 3 each |
| 8 | One-arm pull-up | 1 |

## Timeline, revised

A solid 10-second one-arm lock-off puts a one-arm pull-up much closer than the generic estimate. The lock-off is the top of the range; the hard part of an OAPU is the bottom third, which stages 6 and 7 address directly.

Realistic: stages 4-6 within Block 2 and 3, stage 7 by Block 4 or shortly after, stage 8 plausibly within six to twelve months rather than the 12-24 I estimated when I thought this was starting from scratch.

Still don't chase stage 8 during [[Block 4 — Peak and Send]]. A maximal unilateral attempt inside a send window is a bad trade, and that has nothing to do with the shoulder.

## Progression rule

Advance when the target is hit **on both sides** across two consecutive sessions. Both sides matters — asymmetry is worth tracking on its own.

## Stop rules

- **Pain.** Not the painless click, which lives in underclings and cross-body work rather than here, and has never appeared on bars. Pain is a stop.
- Dead-arm or sudden weakness. Stop, get assessed.
- Medial elbow tenderness. One-arm work loads the medial elbow hard, and this is the most likely injury in this module — more likely than anything shoulder-related given how bar work behaves. Back one stage, double [[Antagonist and Wrist]] for a week.
- Asymmetry over 20% in hold time between sides: work the weak side only until it closes.
- Clicking appearing on bar work, which has always been clean. That's a change in pattern and worth an assessment.

## What is no longer here

The first version gated this module behind six symptom-free weeks of [[Shoulder Protocol]] plus physio clearance, and started it at stage 0. Both were built on an assumed anterior instability that doesn't match how my shoulder actually behaves — bars and edges have never been a problem. Gate removed. [[Shoulder Capacity]] has the correction.
