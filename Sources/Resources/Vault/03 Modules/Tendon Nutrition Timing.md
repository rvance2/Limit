# Tendon Nutrition Timing

One of the better-evidenced supplements in sport, and the timing is the whole thing.

## Protocol

**15 g gelatin or hydrolysed collagen + ~50 mg vitamin C, 30-60 min before loading.**

Then load. The loading does the work; the collagen supplies substrate.

## Why the timing

Baar and Shaw found vitamin C-enriched gelatin taken 30-60 min before exercise roughly doubled markers of collagen synthesis (serum PINP). Amino acids peak in serum around 60 min post-ingestion, so the window of elevated availability has to coincide with the window of loading-stimulated synthesis. After the session, the timing misses.

Vitamin C is a required cofactor for proline and lysine hydroxylation and subsequent collagen crosslinking, which is why it's in the protocol rather than a general health add-on.

Broader range in the literature: 15-30 g hydrolysed collagen with 40-50 mg vitamin C about an hour pre-exercise, outperforming placebo. A 14-week trial of specific collagen peptides alongside high-load resistance training found increased patellar tendon cross-sectional area. A case study of a professional basketball player with patellar tendinopathy used 15 g gelatin with 225 mg vitamin C before heavy isometric loading, normal MRI at 18 months.

## Practical

- Gelatin powder is cheap and works. Hydrolysed collagen dissolves better and tastes less like a hoof.
- Mix in something acidic. Orange juice covers liquid and vitamin C.
- Before [[Max Hangs]], [[MED Hangs]], [[Recruitment Pulls]], and any full-crimp session. Four to five times a week is plenty.
- On a project day, before the drive or the approach, not at the boulder.

## What it won't do

Prevent injury from bad load progression. Fix an acute pulley rupture. Substitute for the [[Tendon Health]] progression ceilings. It changes the rate at which tissue can adapt, which raises my load-progression ceiling by some unknown but non-zero amount.
