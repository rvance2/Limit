# Footwork Precision

Twelve minutes at the start of every [[S4 Volume and Skill]] session, easy terrain. Footwork drilled tired trains sloppy footwork, so this goes first or not at all.

## Rotation

Two drills per session, drawn from the list so the schedule is random rather than blocked. Deliberate — [[Motor Learning]].

1. **Silent feet.** One or two grades below limit. Audible contact = failed rep, restart the boulder.
2. **Target practice.** Pick the exact square centimetre before moving. Eyes on it until the shoe lands. Don't look away early. Hörst's version is the clearest description available.
3. **No-adjust.** Two to three grades below limit. Once the foot lands it stays. No repositioning, no rocking onto it.
4. **Toe tap.** On an overhang, tap the foothold three times before committing weight. Accuracy and toe strength together.
5. **Downclimb everything.** Reverse every boulder I top out. Forces footholds I didn't choose, mostly blind.
6. **Feet-only traverse.** Hands on jugs, deliberately over-load each foot before the hand moves. Fixes "foot is on the hold but doing nothing."
7. **Two-foot minimum.** No hand moves until both feet are weighted and pushing.

## Priority

Downclimbing. High contextual interference — novel positions, no pre-planning, different every time — and the meta-analytic evidence says random practice degrades in-session performance and improves retention and transfer. The trade I want for a skill I need in nine weeks.

Also builds eccentric leg control, free.

## Measurement

Count foot slips per session across the whole session, not just the drill. Log it. A number that halves over six weeks is real progress, and one of the few skill metrics available without motion capture.

## Progression

- Weeks 1-6: 2-3 grades below limit.
- Weeks 7-13: 1-2 grades below limit.
- Weeks 14+: apply the drill *inside* project rehearsal. Silent feet on the crux sequence at 60% speed.
