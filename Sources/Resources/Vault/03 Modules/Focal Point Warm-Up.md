# Focal Point Warm-Up

One technical focal point per session, trained during the warm-up instead of just raising temperature.

Source: [[Burden of Dreams — Wheeler and Shriber]]. The athlete's claim is that these drills, not the strength work, made the crux drop-knee feasible. Tier 3 anecdote, plausible mechanism, cheap to test.

## The idea

A standard warm-up is autopilot — same three boulders, same order, brain elsewhere. That's fifteen to twenty minutes per session, four to five sessions a week, across 22 weeks. Call it 25 hours of climbing with no attentional demand attached.

The intervention: attach one, and only one. "Today my attention is on the outside edge of my right foot." Not a list. One.

Coach's framing was building dependency on something new and increasing frequency of attention. Mine is simpler: I already have to warm up, so the attention is free.

## The drills

Pick one per session. Rotate randomly rather than in order — [[Motor Learning]].

1. **Toe roll.** Hands stay on two holds. Feet on small holds. Move the toes only — roll onto the inside edge, outside edge, big toe, shift weight between feet. Never move the hands. Feel the pressure change direction.
2. **Foothold dwell.** On easy terrain, every foot placement is held for a full three seconds before weighting the next move. Can't flow. Stop, stay, feel.
3. **Heel down, heel up.** Same easy boulder twice, once with heels deliberately dropped, once raised. Notice which holds change character.
4. **Big toe pressure.** Every foot placement, consciously drive the big toe down. This is the [[Footwork]] active-foot idea rehearsed slowly.
5. **Hip proximity.** Every move, hips as close to the wall as the position allows before the hand goes. [[Leverage]].
6. **Silent hands.** Not feet — hands. Place each hand with no slap and no readjustment.
7. **Straight arm.** Every position that can be held with a straight arm, is.
8. **Breath per move.** One breath, one move. Exposes where I rush.
9. **Eyes on target.** Never look away from a hold before the limb lands, hand or foot.
10. **One-foot pressure.** Traverse with deliberate over-loading of one foot before every hand move.

## Dose

Five to eight minutes, inside the existing warm-up, on terrain three or more grades below limit. No added session time.

## Rules

- **One focal point.** Two is zero.
- Below limit, always. A focal point on hard terrain becomes a distraction.
- Name it out loud before starting. Write it in the session log.
- If I can't remember which focal point I used by the end of the session, I didn't use one.

## Relationship to Footwork Precision

[[Footwork Precision]] is a dedicated 12-minute block on skill days with measured outcomes. This is smaller, runs every session, and its content is attention rather than accuracy. They overlap and that's fine — [[Motor Learning]] says the variety is the point.
