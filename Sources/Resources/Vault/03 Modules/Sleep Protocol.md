# Sleep Protocol

Dull, high-return, and the module most likely to get skipped because it doesn't feel like training.

## Targets

| Day type | In bed |
| --- | --- |
| Rest day | 8.5 h |
| Training day | 8.5-9 h |
| Night before a project day | 9 h+ |
| Multi-day trip | 9 h+, plus a 20-30 min nap |

Sleep extension research in athletes suggests extensions of roughly 45-120 min per night produce measurable performance changes. Mah's basketball study (n=11) increased objective nightly sleep ~111 min; sprint 16.2 s → 15.5 s, free throw +9%, three-point +9.2%, PVT reaction time improved. Recommendations for athletes in heavy training land at 8-10 hours.

None of it in climbers. The accuracy and reaction-time findings are the ones most plausibly relevant to catching a hold inside 200 ms. [[Contact Strength]] and [[Precision]].

## Rules

1. **Fixed wake time, every day.** Consistency drives the circadian component harder than total duration. Move bedtime, not wake time.
2. **The night before the night before matters.** Debt on the eve is already priced in. Two good nights beat one, so start two nights out.
3. **No alcohol in the 48 h before a project day.** Fragments sleep architecture, suppresses REM. Performance decision, not moral.
4. **Caffeine cutoff eight hours before bed.** Half-life ~5 h. An afternoon coffee costs the night. Given that 3 mg/kg produced no measurable performance benefit in climbers, trading sleep for it is a bad deal. [[Energy]].
5. **Naps 20-30 min.** Longer risks sleep inertia. Useful on double days, mandatory on trips.
6. **Dark, cool, no screens for the last 30 min.** Standard, works.

## Logging

One number in the morning log: hours in bed, plus 1-5 subjective quality. Feeds [[Readiness Monitoring]].

## The interaction I won't plan for

Sleep quality drops in the days after very heavy sessions. So the hardest weeks are the weeks I sleep worst and the effects compound. One reason the reduced weeks are load-bearing. [[Deload]].
