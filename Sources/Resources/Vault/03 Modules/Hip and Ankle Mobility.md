# Hip and Ankle Mobility

Twelve minutes, three times weekly, unchanged across all 22 weeks. Mobility is maintained, not periodised.

## Why only twelve minutes

Box split distance explains ~2% of grade variance in Lattice's 901-climber dataset, and that's probably inflated because heel-to-heel distance partly measures leg length. Not significant at all within Advanced, Elite, or Higher Elite.

Against that: elite climbers score better on hip flexion range tests than intermediates and non-climbers, and specific moves require specific range. A high step I can't make removes a beta option. A drop knee my hip can't produce gets absorbed by my medial knee.

Enough to keep the option space open, not enough to chase the 2%.

## The session

Hold each 45-60 s, or 8-10 slow reps for dynamic items.

**Hip external rotation** — drop knees, heel hooks, twisting moves.
1. 90/90 seated rotations, both directions.
2. Frog stretch, active, pressing knees down rather than sinking.
3. Pigeon, torso upright.

**Hip flexion and abduction** — high steps and froggers.
4. Deep squat hold, heels down, elbows inside knees.
5. Cossack squats, 8 each side.
6. Standing wall-supported box split, progressing heel-to-heel distance.

**Hip internal rotation** — the neglected one, and what protects my knee in a drop knee.
7. Seated internal rotation, both sides.

**Ankle dorsiflexion** — whether my hips get over a high foot.
8. Knee-to-wall mobilisation, 10 each, measuring toe-to-wall distance.
9. Calf and soleus stretch, bent knee.

## Progression

Measure two things monthly: heel-to-heel box split and toe-to-wall dorsiflexion. Log in [[Test Battery]]. Expect slow change.

## Shin note

Items 8 and 9 also serve [[Lower Limb Resilience]] — limited dorsiflexion and tight soleus are associated with medial tibial stress. Two birds.
