# Style Roulette

Randomised style exposure. Applied contextual interference.

## The evidence

A 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis found high contextual interference improves retention. Random practice schedules produce worse performance during acquisition and better performance on retention and transfer than blocked schedules.

Two mechanisms. Elaboration: switching forces inter-item comparison, enriching encoding. Forgetting-reconstruction: switching forces me to discard and rebuild the motor plan each trial, and the rebuilding is the learning.

A 2025 methodological review is more cautious about generalisability than the enthusiastic reading suggests. Well-supported, not settled.

## Protocol

Eight style cards. Draw at random. One boulder per card, then draw again.

1. Slab or vertical, technical feet
2. 25-30° overhang, crimps
3. 40°+ overhang, big moves
4. Compression, opposing pressure
5. Slopers
6. Coordination or run-and-jump
7. Roof
8. Sit-start power move

Forty minutes, one to two grades below limit. Breadth and unpredictability, not difficulty.

## Rules

- Never two consecutive boulders of the same style. Same card drawn twice → redraw.
- No boulder repeated more than twice in a session. Repetition is blocked practice.
- Accept feeling worse at the end than the start. Feeling more competent means the session was too easy or too blocked. Psychologically unpleasant, worth tolerating.

## Where blocked is correct

Project work. Working a crux is deliberately low-interference because I want acquisition speed, not transfer. [[Limit Bouldering]] and [[Beta Lab]] are blocked on purpose. This is the counterweight.

## Constraint variations

Beyond style, vary constraints. Same boulder no left hand. Same boulder feet only on smears. Same boulder must match on every hold. The practice-variability literature supports varying constraints as well as tasks.
