# Deadpoint Power

My campus board substitute. Bounded dynamic moves on gym holds where the catch is the crux.

## Why this instead

The campus board study is the best power evidence in climbing: sixteen advanced-to-elite climbers, five weeks, volume matched. Four weekly sessions improved RFD and maximal force. Two improved bouldering performance and moves-to-failure. Their exercises were laddering, 1-4-7-10, a 1-2-3 drill, and a ten-move near-max set.

No campus board, so the exercise changes and the dose principle carries: two sessions per week, block periodised, because four sessions of explosive work leaves me unable to climb well.

## Dose

- 4-6 movement patterns, 3-4 reps each.
- 90 s between reps, 3 min between patterns.
- Maximum intent every rep. This is [[Rate of Force Development]] work and it dies at 90% effort.
- Twice weekly in [[Block 3 — Power and RFD]].
- 25-35 min.

## Progression order

1. **Bumps.** Hand to hold, immediately to the next, no settling. Two-hand contact throughout. Safest, start here.
2. **Deadpoints on incuts.** Move to a hold at the top of the arc, momentarily weightless. Soft catch. Both feet on.
3. **Deadpoints on flat edges.** Harder catch.
4. **Foot-cut deadpoints.** Feet come off on the catch.
5. **Double-hand bumps.** Both hands move.
6. **Full dynos.** Block 3 onward.

## On the shoulder

Version one excluded dynos and one-arm catches and kept both feet on until week 14. That came from an assumed anterior instability. Catching on bars and edges has never produced symptoms — [[Shoulder Capacity]] has the correction — so those restrictions are gone.

The remaining order is general load management: catches are high-impulse and the tissue that actually limits them is finger, not shoulder. Advance on clean landings, not on a calendar.

## Trajectory before force

Before adding power to a catch I can't stick, lower the start position. A longer ascending phase buys milliseconds in which the hand is closing while the body is still rising, and improves the angle of pull. [[Contact Strength]] has the reasoning.

Worth trying on every rep here, since the whole module is catches: initiate lower than feels natural and see whether the catch improves without any change in effort.

## Stop rules

- Shoulder **pain**, as distinct from the painless positional click, which doesn't appear here. Over for the day.
- Any finger pop, click, or sting. Session over.
- Landing quality degrading — sloppy catches, uncontrolled swings — means the neural system is fatigued and the exercise is finished regardless of reps remaining.
