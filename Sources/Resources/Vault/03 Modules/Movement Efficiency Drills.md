# Movement Efficiency Drills

Fifteen minutes, once or twice weekly. Also the primary content of reduced weeks, when I'm climbing 2-3 grades below limit anyway and need something worth doing.

## Metrics I can actually use

Research metrics are geometric entropy of the centre of mass and jerk of the hip trajectory, both needing motion capture. Field versions:

- **Hip path trace.** Film side-on. Trace the path on screen with a finger. Compare total path length to vertical distance gained. Wandering, looping and lateral excursion are transport costs.
- **Acceleration count.** Count speed-ups and slow-downs within single moves. Each one is jerk.
- **Immobile time.** Time spent stationary. Counterintuitively more is often better; the question is whether the stillness is functional (holding a position) or dysfunctional (hesitating).

## Drills

1. **Minimum moves.** Familiar boulder, fewest hand movements possible. Punishes readjustment and intermediates I didn't need.
2. **Straight-arm audit.** Consciously seek straight arms wherever possible. A bent arm is a decision to spend biceps on a position skeleton could hold.
3. **Hip-to-wall.** Every move, hips as close to the wall as the position allows before the hand moves. Directly reduces the moment my fingers resist. [[Leverage]].
4. **Eyes-closed opening.** First two moves of an easy boulder with eyes closed after reading it. Forces proprioceptive precision.
5. **No-match.** No matching hands on any hold. Exposes lazy sequencing.
6. **Slow-motion crux.** Project crux at 50% speed, as far as slow is possible. Wherever slow becomes impossible is where I'm relying on momentum, which is information.

## Why this is the reduced-week module

Reduced weeks are four to five days on terrain 2-3 grades below limit — precisely the terrain where efficiency work is possible, because I have spare capacity to attend to *how* rather than *whether*. Reduced weeks are when my technique actually improves.

## Progression

Film the same benchmark boulder in week 1, week 12, and week 22. Compare hip paths. Closest thing to objective skill measurement without a lab, and more informative than I expect.
