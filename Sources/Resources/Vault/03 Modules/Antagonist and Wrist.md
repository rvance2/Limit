# Antagonist and Wrist

Eight minutes, twice weekly. Prophylactic, boring, and why my elbows survive twenty-two weeks of finger training.

No climbing-specific trial supports this. The rationale is imbalance: heavy flexor loading with no extensor work is the standard precursor to lateral elbow tendinopathy in climbers, and wrist position affects forearm load distribution during gripping.

## The session

1. **Wrist extension**, light dumbbell or band. 3 × 15, slow eccentric. Highest-value item here.
2. **Wrist flexion.** 3 × 15. Both directions, not just the neglected one.
3. **Pronation and supination** with a hammer or light bar. 2 × 12 each.
4. **Reverse wrist curls** over a bench edge. 2 × 15.
5. **Finger extension** against a band around all five digits. 3 × 20.
6. **Push-ups or dips, 2 × 10.** Pressing balance for all the pulling. Keep dips shallow — no deeper than 90° at the elbow.
7. **Rice bucket or putty, 2 min.** Optional, pleasant, probably does something.

## Wrist position

Wrist position during gripping changes forearm and elbow load distribution. A collapsed, flexed wrist during hangs shifts load in ways associated with discomfort. During [[Max Hangs]] and [[MED Hangs]], keep the wrist near neutral rather than letting it extend or flex under load.

## Progression

Slowly. Insurance, not a development target. If it makes me sore enough to affect finger sessions, it's too heavy.

## Elbow warning signs

- Tenderness on the lateral epicondyle after sessions: double extensor volume, cut finger volume 20% for a week.
- Pain gripping a doorknob or a full mug: established tendinopathy. Reduce load and get it looked at. Months to resolve if I train through it.
